How

  1. Each photograph squared up from its own vanishing points (smc.facades.photo_rectify).
  2. The front panel cut out and given only its width and height in metres, read from the drawing's scale bar -- the priors the footprint and the lidar give in the city.
  3. The pipeline unchanged: segmentation, detector, extract_front.
  4. Openings marked on the drawing and matched to the predicted ones (centres within max(1.5 m, the opening's size), overlap IoU >= 0.1).

What it shows

  • Height is where it fails. Inferred from the squared-up photograph, the front came out within 15 per cent of the drawing only at Larkin (6.7 m against 7.8 m); Carson 7.3 against 4.9, and the rest 2-4.6 times too tall (Banning 19.7 against 10.0, the Governor's Mansion 25.7 against 12.3, Stanford 26.7 against 13.7, Amesti 30.6 against 6.7, J. S. Cain 29.4 against 7.3): the panel took in sky, trees and ground, and the openings landed in the wrong places. Larkin, where it held, scored 6 of 10 with no false openings and a 0.32 m position error.
  • The footprint's width is right when the elevation is one wall (Larkin 0.53 m out, Amesti 0.02 m) and wrong when the facing side steps (Banning 25.8 m of walls against a 15.5 m front, Carson 17.9 against 11.3, J. S. Cain 26.3 against 14.8): wings, porches and bays are summed into it.
  • Fusing photographs does worse than the single best one: an oblique view with a wrong extent outvotes the square-on one.
  • So the city pipeline's lidar height and its pose-projected wall, not the photograph alone, are what make the 2026-10-12 figures possible; a house whose wall height is not known should not be reconstructed from its photograph's aspect.

How

  1. Given per house: one to three photographs (as many as the survey has of that front, at most three) and the OpenStreetMap footprint. Nothing from the drawings: no width, no height, no panel cut-out.
  2. Each photograph squared up from its own vanishing points; the focal length from two vanishing points, else from the front's and a side wall's horizontals, else a view-camera prior (1.4 x the long side).
  3. The front's extent found in the photograph (segmentation, then the longest run of level lines: eaves, string courses).
  4. Width: the footprint's walls facing the survey's elevation. Height: inferred from the squared-up photograph's aspect, as the city pipeline would without lidar.
  5. The pipeline unchanged: segmentation, detector, extract_front.
  6. Scored as on 2026-10-12: a predicted opening matches a drawn one when their centres are within max(1.5 m, the opening's size) and they overlap (IoU >= 0.1).
  7. The photograph scored is the one the frontage filter would pick (within 40 degrees, nearest square on, then the largest share of the frame); a fusion of all the house's photographs is reported beside it.

  • Lovell Beach House, Newport Beach (HABS CAL,30-NEWBE,1) — the west front is continuous ribbon glazing on stilts: no discrete openings to match one to one
  • Yung See San Fong, Los Gatos (HABS CAL,43-LOSGA,2) — every photograph looks steeply along the drive, beyond 40 degrees to any drawn front
  • Sowden House, Los Angeles (HABS CAL,19-LOSAN,69) — no OSM building contains its address; the footprint would have to be guessed
  • Harrison Street Block, Santa Clara (HABS CAL,43-SANCLA,2) — no addressed OSM footprints for 1009 or 1077; the houses would have to be guessed
  • Casa del Rancho San Antonio, Bell Gardens (HABS CAL,19-BEL,1) — the nearest OSM building is 38 m from the address and its east front's sheet would not download whole
  • D. V. Cain House, Bodie — excluded by request

What it shows

  • Lidar gets the height the photograph could not: within 6 per cent of the drawing at Larkin (7.34 m against 7.77), Carson (5.15 against 4.93) and J. S. Cain (6.97 against 7.34); 31-40 per cent over where a cupola, a tower or a tree rises above the drawn front (Banning 13.94 against 10.03, the Governor's Mansion 17.24 against 12.30, Stanford 17.97 against 13.71, Amesti 9.1 against 6.65). The first run's photograph-aspect heights were 2-4.6 times too tall.
  • But height was not what held the score down. With lidar heights, 16 of 113 drawn openings were found (43 per cent of predictions real), against 13 with heights from the photograph; and given the drawing's own width and height, still 16. The loss is in finding the front in an archival photograph and in the detector's proposals on it: about 31-37 openings proposed for 113 drawn, and the two large mansions (69 of the 113) yield 5-10 each.
  • Larkin, a plain two-storey front squarely photographed, holds in every blind run: 6 of 10 found, none false, 0.3-0.5 m position error.
  • On 2026-10-12 the same pipeline found 80 per cent of openings when the front was cut out of the photograph by hand. In the city that cut comes from the camera's recorded pose projected onto the footprint, not from finding the house in the picture, so that figure is the closer guide for the city -- where the pose is right; this test is the guide for photographs without a pose.
  • Fusing photographs still does worse than the filter's single photograph: the oblique views disagree with the square one.

How

  1. Given per house: one to three photographs (as many as the survey has of that front, at most three), the OpenStreetMap footprint, and the lidar. Nothing from the drawings.
  2. Height: smc.lidar.region.building_height, unchanged -- the roof's 92nd percentile over the ground at the house's foot, from the newest 3DEP collection covering it (all seven are covered).
  3. Width: the footprint's walls facing the survey's elevation, end to end.
  4. Each photograph squared up from its own vanishing points; the house found as Grounding DINO boxes it (prompt 'house. building.', the most confident box across the frame's middle); its ground line is the box's foot, its ends the box's sides, and the lidar height laid up from the ground line at the squared-up photograph's own scale.
  5. The pipeline unchanged: segmentation, detector, extract_front. Scored as before (centres within max(1.5 m, the opening's size), IoU >= 0.1), on the photograph the frontage filter would pick, with a fusion of all the house's photographs beside it.
  6. A diagnostic run, not blind, gives the same photographs the drawing's own width and height, to tell how much of the loss is size and how much is finding the front and its openings.
