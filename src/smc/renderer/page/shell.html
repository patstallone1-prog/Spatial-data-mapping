          Getting around

          Click to move without changing zoom. Drag to look around (you keep walking while you do), use arrow keys to walk, press F for first person, S for what is under the pointer, Enter to open a door or gate, 1, 2 or 3 to drop a pin (again to go back to it, then Delete to remove it), and wheel or pinch to zoom.

          Mouse and touch

          Click / tap the ground
          Walk there. The zoom stays as it is.
          Drag
          Look around. You keep walking while you do.
          Wheel / pinch
          Zoom in and out.
          Click a billboard
          Opens what it advertises.
          Click a region's marker
          Flies you to that region.

          Keyboard

          ↑ ↓
          Walk forward and back.
          ← →
          Step left and right.
          F
          First person: see out of the walker's eyes, and back out.
          E
          Explore: the walker is put away and the arrows move the view anywhere; press again and the walker stands where you are.
          Enter
          Open or close the nearest door or gate.
          S
          What is under the pointer: a building's address, or a street's name and its cross streets. With the map panel closed.
          1 2 3
          Drop that pin on the ground under the pointer. Once it is down, the same key takes you back to it.
          Shift + 1 2 3
          Move that pin to the pointer.
          R
          Back to where you are: the walker to your location, following you again. Moving the walker yourself lets it go until then.
          Esc
          Close settings, or the address card.

          Keys do nothing to the map while you are typing in the search or directions boxes.

          Directions

          Routes are computed by Valhalla on the legal OpenStreetMap street network (© OpenStreetMap contributors), served by FOSSGIS; transit by Transitous from the agencies' published timetables. The stops you enter are sent to those servers. Times are typical or scheduled, without live traffic. Never a path over what this map draws.

          Each route is tagged where it is clearly better than the fastest at something: Fewest hills, Avoids stairs, Bike lane friendly, Fewest turns, Least walking, Fewest transfers. A small difference earns no tag. A tag also moves a route up the list by how much it saves -- 300 ft less climbing counts about 6% of a bike ride's time.
