### Notes on reading these figures

- **Footprint precision.** `footprint` rounds whole-GB values, so "10 GB" (10,240 MB) means about 10 GB.
- **Footprint against RSS.** Footprint counts compressed and swapped pages, and RSS does not. At its peak, WebContent RSS was only 2.9 to 3.9 GB. In the warm run's steady state, the dirty, resident categories added up to 3.2 GB, against a footprint of 5.8 GB. So roughly 2.6 GB of the page was compressed or in swap.
  - On this machine, swap lives on the nearly full internal disk. System swap use was 2.3 GB of 3 GB just after the cold run.
- **Extra processes in the cold run.** The cold run's first fallback matcher briefly picked up other apps' WebKit helpers: 3 WebContent processes at 200.8 s, and 2 Networking processes at 274.9 to 276.9 s. The cold peaks above are not affected, because they fall at samples where exactly one process of each kind was counted. measure.py was tightened before the warm run, which counted exactly one process of each kind.
- **Live count.** `people` stayed at 0 for about 30 s after the lobby went in both runs. `kerbsideCity().visible()` returned empty arrays until the crowd had spawned, at 172.7 s cold and 166.3 s warm.

### CPU

- While loading, the web content process averaged 108 to 114% of a core, with peaks of 173 to 177%.
- In the city, it averaged 38 to 48%. The GPU process averaged 3 to 11%, with a 60% peak during "Setting the tree pits". The Networking and app processes were negligible.

### Frame rate

The city ran at 4.7 fps, with frames of about 200 to 270 ms, in both runs, locked and unlocked. Each frame drew 930 to 1,000 draw calls, 11 to 12 million triangles and 262 to 279 programs.

The main thread was not saturated during this (WebContent 27 to 48% of a core in the city), and the GPU process used about 3% CPU. So the frame time is spent **waiting**, not computing in JS. The likely suspects are GPU execution, or synchronous WebGL round trips to the GPU process: in WebKit, WebGL runs out of process through ANGLE/Metal, so calls such as getParameter, getError and readPixels block on IPC. Neither of these was measured, so this is a hypothesis to test next, not a finding.

For comparison, memory notes from 2026-11-11 (Electron/Chrome, not re-measured here) give 24 to 42 fps at 476 draws in the Marina.

### Missing APIs and what each costs the game

- **`performance.memory`: absent.** The desktop memory governor (module 28: `if (!META && !ATTACH && performance.memory) setInterval(desktopMemoryTick, ...)`) is never scheduled on WebKit.
  - Every one of the cold run's 274 samples read `{level: "ok", sheds: 0, heap: null}`. Nothing trims the cell or tile caches.
  - WebKit has no 4 GiB V8-style heap ceiling: WebContent reached ~10 GB with no termination. So the room is there, but nothing holds the page to a budget, and the overflow goes to compression and swap.
- **`performance.measureUserAgentSpecificMemory`: absent** (and it needs crossOriginIsolated anyway). There is no browser-side heap figure for a replacement governor. It would have to use the page's own byte accounting, like the META path's estimate (`cells + tileResidentBytes + 300 MiB`).
- **`navigator.deviceMemory`: null.** 00-boot.js only shrinks the stream when it is present and below 8, so WebKit gets the full desktop stream settings. There is no cost, but also no signal.
- **`navigator.gpu` (WebGPU): absent** in this WKWebView. The page does not use it (no reference in the page modules), so there is no cost today.
- **SharedArrayBuffer and crossOriginIsolated: false.** The site sends no COOP/COEP, and the page does not use SharedArrayBuffer, so there is no cost today. It would block shared-memory workers.
- **Present:** WebGL2 (Apple GPU, WebGL 2.0, 36 extensions, MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE 16384), pointer lock, OffscreenCanvas with WebGL2.
- **Storage:** `navigator.storage.estimate()` gave usage 0 and quota 20.6 GB; `persisted()` was false.

### What the numbers suggest optimising (ranked)

1. **Frame time in the city (4.7 fps).** Measure first where the ~210 ms goes, using a per-frame count of synchronous WebGL calls and GPU timer queries if WebKit exposes them (not probed).
   - The work per frame is high regardless: ~1,000 draws, ~12 M triangles and ~270 programs. Fewer draws and triangles, and fewer program switches, is what moved fps in Chrome before.
2. **The people-and-traffic spawn burst (+4.7 GB in 4 s, peak ~10 GB).** Spread spawning over frames, pool or pre-size the per-agent buffers, and avoid throwaway arrays. This single spike sets the peak in both runs and drops fps to 0.4.
3. **A memory governor that works without `performance.memory`.** Drive it from the page's own accounting (cells, tiles, geometry and texture bytes), so WebKit gets a budget too. Today it is off, steady footprint is 5.8 to 8 GB, and a few GB of that is in compression or swap on a nearly full disk.
4. **"Loading the Marina, the streets round you" (about 70 s, +3 GB).** This is the bulk of the load time and of the load's memory growth. Since cache made little difference, the cost is in building, not fetching.
5. **Typed-array memory (Gigacage 0.9 to 1.2 GB).** CPU-side copies of geometry are kept after upload. Releasing static attribute arrays once they are on the GPU would cut this. That is an inference from the category; which arrays are held was not measured.
6. **Disk is not a problem.** The app is 240 KB against Electron's 289 MB, and website data is 186 MB of HTTP cache plus about 1.5 MB of site data.
