The map

Today it is a street map. It fills in as people walk.

Every footway on this map is a place the network can eventually describe: kerb height, width, surface, whether a ramp exists and whether it is passable. Almost none of it is described yet — that is the honest state of a map built from scratch. Pink shows where street-level imagery already exists. As scans accumulate, those segments carry measured geometry, and dense corridors become continuous three-dimensional reconstructions rather than lines on a plan.

The capture app

Get the app

It listens for the moments worth photographing, keeps only the frames that carry information, compresses them overnight and sends one batch a day. What it can do depends on the hardware it runs on, and the differences are not small.

    The iPhone limitation, stated plainly

    iOS will not let any app use the camera while it is in the background. That is a platform rule, not something an app can request an exception to, and no capture app on the App Store works around it. On iPhone you shoot manually, or you pair glasses and the phone becomes the brains rather than the eyes.

    Everything after the shutter still runs automatically on iPhone: scoring, culling, compression and the nightly send all work in the background.

    What runs overnight, on your phone

    At 2am, on a charger and on Wi-Fi, the app reviews the day: it drops frames that are dark, blown out, blurred, near-duplicates of a sharper frame, or surplus to an area already well covered — and deletes any frame whose subject is a person before anything is sent.

    What survives is re-encoded and uploaded once. Local copies are erased only after the server confirms receipt. On a real day's photographs that is roughly a fifty-fold reduction, and one transfer instead of a radio kept warm all day.

    How it works

    From a photograph to a measurement

    1 · Capture what matters

    A frame is taken when you have moved far enough for it to show something new — not on a timer. Walking pace varies constantly; distance does not, so the spacing between frames stays even whether you dawdle or stride.

    2 · Work out where it was taken

    GPS puts a photograph on roughly the right block, several metres out. Matching it against surveyed reference imagery of the same place recovers position and heading to within centimetres. This is the step everything else depends on.

    3 · Measure, and say how sure

    Overlapping views give kerb height, footway width and surface. Every value carries a confidence, whether it was measured or inferred, and how recently it was seen. Where a measurement contradicts the plan, the measurement wins and the disagreement is flagged.
