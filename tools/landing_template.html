The street, measured.

Kerbside is a 3D model of the city built only out of things somebody measured. Kerbs read off public lidar. Walls coloured from street-level photographs. Ground, parcels and curb lines from the cities' own records. Where nothing was measured, it says so rather than inventing a street.

How it works

Four public sources, none of them guessed

Every surface in the model traces back to a measurement with a name and a date. Nothing is modelled by hand and nothing is filled in to look better than it is.

01

Lidar reads the kerb

USGS aerial lidar, cell by cell. The kerb is the riser between gutter and footway, found in the returns every four metres along each street, with outliers dropped rather than smoothed away.

02

Photographs give the wall

Street-level imagery from Mapillary, KartaView and Panoramax is rectified against each building's face, and the wall's colour is the median over the views that actually see it.

03

Cities give the ground

Parcels, parks, street trees and curb lines come from each city's own portal — DataSF, Oakland, Palo Alto's ArcGIS. Where a city publishes nothing, that is recorded as an absence.

04

The map gives the network

OpenStreetMap supplies what the street is: which ways exist, where the crossings and footways run, and the benches, bus stops and signs along them. Its widths are treated as a starting point, not as a survey.

The detail

Down to the crossing and the kerb ramp

The things a map usually leaves out

Continental crossing bars where the city marks them and transverse lines where it does not. Kerb ramps with their detectable warning surfaces. Loading zones in the colour the city paints them. Pavement scored into five-foot slabs, each poured a slightly different shade.

Measured and inferred are kept apart everywhere. A street whose width came from a class prior is recorded as inferred, not drawn as though somebody had been out with a tape.

A whole city, block by block

Back gardens come from the parcel lines, with fences on the boundaries neighbours share. House colours are sampled off the photographs of those houses, not chosen from a palette — and a house nobody has photographed keeps its archetype and is counted as unsampled.

__COLOURS__ walls have a colour read off a photograph; __BUILDINGS__ buildings are drawn in total, and the difference is published rather than hidden.

Coverage

Eight areas, and honest edges

San Francisco's Marina-to-Financial-District corridor, the Mission, the Sunset and Haight–Castro; downtown Oakland with the Alameda tube approaches; downtown Berkeley, San Jose and Palo Alto. Zoom out and the whole Bay Area is one model.
