Kerbside

A map of the walkable world

The pavement is
missing from the map.

Every navigation system knows the roads to the centimetre and almost nothing about the three feet beside them. Kerbside builds that missing layer from ordinary photographs — how high the kerb is, how wide the footway, whether the ramp is really passable — captured by people already walking past.

How it works

Depth without a depth sensor.

01

It photographs when you move

Not on a timer. A frame is taken once you have walked far enough for it to show something new, so the spacing between frames holds whether you dawdle or stride — and that even spacing is what lets two views be turned into geometry.

02

It works out where you stood

A phone's GPS is accurate to several metres, which is the width of the entire footway. Matching what the camera saw against surveyed imagery of the same place recovers the position and the direction you faced, to within centimetres.

03

It measures, and admits doubt

Overlapping views give kerb height, footway width and surface. Every figure carries how confident it is and when it was last seen. Where a measurement contradicts the plan, the measurement wins and the disagreement is recorded.

On your phone

Most of what it photographs, it throws away.

Uploading everything would flatten a battery and a data plan for almost no return. So the judging happens where the photographs already are.

Nightly, on a charger

At two in the morning the app reviews the day and drops what carries no information: too dark, blown out, blurred, near-identical to a sharper frame, or surplus to a place already well covered.

People are deleted, not blurred

A photograph whose subject is a person says nothing about a kerb. Those frames are deleted on the device before anything is compressed or sent. Where the phone cannot judge automatically, it asks you, and sends nothing unless you say so.

One batch, then erased

What survives is compressed and sent once. Local copies are erased only after the batch is safely written — until then your phone holds the only copy, and it is treated that way.

Nothing is uploaded without you.

Photographs live in the app's own storage on your device. The nightly run produces a batch that you choose to save. There is no silent background upload, and no account.
